# How to grow website traffic in Slovakia: 7 sources with no ad budget

The question is usually put as "how do I raise traffic". Badly put: traffic is not the goal. A thousand random visits from Indonesia are worth less than twenty from Bratislava by people who need your service.

Below are seven sources, each of which can be started with no advertising budget. Sorted by speed of return, not by volume.

## 1. A Google Business Profile — the fastest

If you have an address or work in a specific city, this is the first thing to do.

The listing appears on maps and in the local block above the organic results. It does not have to wait for the site to be indexed or for the domain to gain weight — it works almost immediately.

What actually affects its position:

- **Category.** You choose one primary category, and it determines which queries you compete on at all.
- **Reviews.** The main factor in the local block. Eight to twelve honest reviews from real clients change the picture more than any text on the site.
- **Regular posts.** One a week signals that the business is alive.
- **Photographs.** Real ones, not stock.

**Realistic timescale:** first calls in about two to three weeks.

## 2. Directories with a Slovak NAP

NAP means name, address, phone. The rule is simple: they must match **word for word** everywhere there is a record of you. Discrepancies in the address are the most common reason local visibility stalls.

Slovak directories worth adding yourself to: `firmy.sk`, `zlatestranky.sk`, `superfirmy.sk`, `azet.sk`, `edb.sk`, `foaf.sk`, `finstat.sk`.

Each entry gives a link and confirms the company exists. Individually trivial, together a noticeable signal.

**Realistic timescale:** a one-off job over a couple of evenings, with a cumulative effect.

## 3. Communities: the most underrated channel

For a Russian-speaking entrepreneur in Slovakia this is arguably the most effective free source — and the most inconvenient, because it demands your personal involvement.

The mechanism: in Facebook groups and Telegram chats of Russian-speakers in Slovakia, people constantly ask about business, documents, websites, payments, cash registers. A substantive answer to such a question is reputation and traffic at once.

The rules, without which it does not work:

- **Answer, do not advertise.** Give a link only where it genuinely helps.
- **Answer fully.** "Message me privately" does not work and irritates people.
- **Regularly.** Two or three answers a week produce an effect; one a month does not.

And a side benefit worth more than the traffic: **every question asked is a ready topic for a page.** It is the most accurate source of keywords in existence, because it is the literal wording your audience uses.

## 4. Links you already have

If you make things for clients — websites, renovations, signage — you have access to places where a link to you belongs.

A line in the footer of a client's site reading "Website by …" is a relevant link from a Slovak domain in the same field. Exactly what competitors pay money for.

Twelve client sites means twelve such links. An evening's work, if you have the access.

## 5. Content that answers questions

The slowest source and the longest-lasting.

One rule: **a page is written for a specific question, not for a topic.** "Everything about business in Slovakia" will never be found. "When you must register for VAT" will be.

What works in the Slovak context:

- questions people ask before buying: timescales, prices, what is included;
- questions about local rules: company details, cash registers, taxes, cookies;
- comparisons: one solution against another;
- post-mortems: what goes wrong and why.

**Realistic timescale:** first positions in 2–3 months, noticeable traffic in 4–6, at 20–30 pages.

## 6. Partners with the same clients

The scheme that works best in Slovakia and is barely used.

Your client has an accountant, a lawyer, a relocation agency, an estate agent, a translator. They have the same client but a different service — there is no competition and a recommendation is natural.

Five to eight such partners produce a flow of enquiries independent of both Google and budget. And unlike advertising, it does not stop when you stop paying.

The easiest place to start is with people you have already worked alongside.

## 7. Email and repeat contact

The most underused resource is the people who have already bought from you, or enquired and did not buy.

They need no explanation of who you are. A letter once a month with something useful — a change in the rules, a common mistake explained — keeps you in view. When the need arises they will remember you rather than the search results.

## What not to do

Three things that waste time and sometimes cause harm:

**Buying links.** Against the rules, and on a small Slovak market conspicuous as well.

**Chasing traffic volume.** The visitor count means nothing by itself. Count enquiries — and for that you first need [measurement configured](/en/blog/ga4-setup/).

**Writing "for SEO".** A text stuffed with repeated keywords is closed after a paragraph, and search sees that in the behaviour.

## Where to start this week

Trying to do everything at once means doing nothing. The order:

1. Tidy up the Google listing: category, photographs, description.
2. Ask five clients you know are happy for reviews.
3. Add yourself to three Slovak directories with an identical NAP.
4. Put links in the footers of sites you have access to.
5. Pick two communities and start answering.
6. Write the first page — on the question you are asked most often.
