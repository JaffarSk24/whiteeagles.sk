# How Slovaks choose a contractor: what a website needs for people to trust you

Between "someone found your site" and "someone wrote to you" a few minutes pass, and the decision is made in them. They do not read your texts through — they check off a list: do you exist, can you handle this, and what will it cost.

That list is fairly consistent, and it makes an easy test for your own site.

## First: does the company exist

Before assessing your work, a person makes sure you are real. They look at the footer.

**IČO, address, register entry details.** Their absence does not read as a detail — it reads as a reason not to take the risk. Especially where a deposit is involved.

The full list of what belongs there is in [a separate article](/en/blog/mandatory-website-details/).

The same applies to email on your own domain. A message from `info@yourdomain.sk` and one from `company2026@gmail.com` read differently, and the difference costs nothing.

## Second: have you done this before

A "portfolio" section of logos barely works. What works is **specific examples**: which company, what the task was, what you did, what came of it.

Ideally with a link to the live site. The client will follow it and see for themselves. If there is no link, the question is why.

An example **from the same industry** carries particular force. A beauty salon picks the person who has already built a site for a salon, not the one with forty projects across unrelated fields.

## Third: what it costs

This is where clients are most often lost for no good reason.

"Price on request" reads as "this will be expensive, but first we will work on you". Some people simply close the tab rather than enter negotiations.

Something else works: **the order of magnitude and an explanation of what it depends on.** "From this much; the final figure depends on the number of pages, integrations and the booking system" is honest and removes the main fear — that the price will turn out to be unpredictable.

You do not have to publish an exact price. It is enough to let a person work out whether they fall inside your range.

## Fourth: what other people say

Reviews work, but not just any reviews.

**An anonymous "Great work, recommended!" weighs nothing.** Anyone could have written it, including you.

**A review naming a company with a link to its site weighs a lot.** It can be verified.

**Reviews on a Google profile weigh most of all**, because you cannot write them yourself. For local services this is often the deciding argument between two similar contractors.

And an honest note: review markup on your own site without real, verifiable reviews is a bad idea. Google ignores such markup, and may treat it as spam.

## Fifth: how fast you reply

A Slovak client writes more often than they call. Two practical consequences follow.

**The form must work flawlessly.** Test it once a month with a real submission. A form that pretends to have sent while the email never arrives is a very common failure, and the owner is the last to find out.

**Reply the same day.** Someone who wrote to three people will choose the one who answered first and to the point. A day's delay is usually a loss.

## Sixth: how the site looks on a phone

More than half of people will open you on a phone. If the text has to be pinch-zoomed and the "Send" button runs off the edge, you lost them before they finished the first paragraph.

## Seventh: does anyone find you at all

A separate layer, and the last one people think about. Even a flawless site does nothing if nobody reaches it.

For local services the **Google Business Profile** is often the first point of contact — before the site. It is free and filling it in takes an evening.

Plus local directories: Firmy.sk, Azet, Zlaté stránky. The data in them must match the site to the character, otherwise Google links your company into a single entity less reliably.

## Checklist

Open your own site on a phone in a private window and check:

1. Are the IČO, address and registration details visible without searching?
2. Are there at least three specific examples of work, with links?
3. Is the order of the price clear?
4. Are there verifiable reviews — with names and links?
5. Does the form work? Send a test enquiry right now.
6. Is all of this comfortable on a small screen?
7. Is your Google profile filled in, and does it have reviews?

Every item that does not check out is people who reached the site and left.
