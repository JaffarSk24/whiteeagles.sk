# PPC advertising: what it is, how it works and what a Google campaign costs

PPC stands for pay per click. You pay only when someone clicks the ad and lands on your website; being shown is free. In Slovakia PPC usually means advertising in Google Ads, and in everyday speech also ads on Facebook and Instagram.

Below: how PPC advertising works, which campaign types exist, what a click and a budget cost in real figures from my campaigns, and when PPC does not pay off at all. ## What PPC advertising is and how it differs from banners

Traditional advertising is paid for space: a billboard, a newspaper article, a banner on a portal for a month. You pay whether anyone reacted or not.

PPC advertising is paid for the reaction. Google may show the ad a thousand times and charge only for those who clicked. On top of that, the ad is seen by someone who is searching for what you sell right now, typing "sťahovanie bratislava" or "terasové dosky cena" into Google. That is why search PPC brings people with a ready need rather than random viewers.

The second difference is measurement. With PPC you know exactly what a click cost, how many clickers filled in the form or called, and what one enquiry cost. With a billboard you never know.

## How PPC advertising works in Google

Every search triggers an auction. Everyone who wants to show an ad for that search takes part, and Google decides on two things:

- **the bid**: how much you are willing to pay per click;
- **the quality score**: how well your ad and the page it leads to match what the person searched for.

The practical consequence: whoever has the better ad and the better page pays less for the same position. A slow website with generic text instead of a page for the specific service costs you twice, through a higher price per click and through clicks that never become enquiries. The cost per click is covered in detail in [what Google Ads costs in Slovakia](/en/blog/google-ads-cost-slovakia/).

Keywords decide which searches show the ad. **Negative keywords** matter just as much: the searches where it must not show. A campaign for "sklad bratislava" without negatives also pays for "práca sklad bratislava", and those people want a job, not a rental.

## PPC campaign types

| Campaign | Where the ad appears | Who it suits | What you pay for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Search** | a text ad above Google results | services and products people search for in words | a click |
| **Shopping** | a product with photo and price in Google results | online shops with a catalogue | a click |
| **Performance Max** | everywhere in Google at once: search, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, websites | shops and companies with sales tracking in place | a click |
| **Display (banners)** | image ads on websites and in apps | events, new brands, reminders | a click or impressions |
| **YouTube** | video before and during videos | brands that can show the product in motion | a view or impressions |
| **Meta (Facebook, Instagram)** | in feeds, stories and reels | products people need to notice before they search for them | usually a thousand impressions |

For most small companies in Slovakia the starting point is clear: **search**, because that is where people are already looking. An online shop adds [Google Shopping](/en/blog/google-shopping-for-online-shops/), which is often the most profitable campaign in the account. How to choose between Google and Meta when the budget covers only one is in [Google Ads or Meta](/en/blog/google-ads-or-meta/). How Meta ads work and what they cost is in [Facebook and Instagram ads](/en/blog/facebook-instagram-ads-cost/).

A click on its own says nothing. What matters is the cost of an enquiry or a sale. At Severské drevo every euro of advertising produced 7.47€ in orders, and the Shopping campaign alone 14.18€.

**The budget** is worked out from the enquiry, not from what you "don't mind" spending. One service in one city needs 10-15€ a day, or 300-450€ a month: enough to collect 20-30 contacts in a month and decide whether the campaign continues. You pay the budget straight to Google or Meta from your own account.

**The work** is separate: a complete campaign launch costs about 1,000€ once with me, management 25€/hr for the time actually spent, with no lock-in. In Slovakia you will also meet a percentage of the budget (usually 10-20%) or a fixed monthly fee.

## PPC or SEO

It is not a question of "or" but of "when".

**PPC** brings visitors from the first day. Stop paying and the visitors stop too. It is a variable cost.

**SEO** takes three to six months to build up, but then brings people without paying per click. It is an investment in a website you own.

A small company usually needs both: PPC for enquiries now and SEO so that a year later it does not have to buy every visit. PPC also shows within a few weeks which words actually bring customers, and the website pages are then written around them.

## When a PPC campaign pays off and when it does not

It pays off when:

- **people search for your service.** The Keyword Planner in Google Ads shows for free how many people in Slovakia search for your words each month. "tvorba web stránok" (website development), for example, gets about 2,400 searches, "ppc reklamy" and "pay per click reklama" 480 each;
- **you have a page for the specific service.** Sending ads to the home page is the most common cause of expensive enquiries;
- **you track enquiries**: forms, calls, purchases. Without tracking Google has nothing to optimise for and spends on the cheapest clicks;
- **you have a reserve for two or three months.** The first weeks go on learning and collecting negative keywords.

It does not pay off when almost nobody searches for your service. The money then belongs in other channels; [how to grow website traffic](/en/blog/grow-website-traffic/) shows which. Nor does it pay off when the margin cannot carry the cost of a customer: if a customer costs 300€ and brings 400€, the problem is not the advertising.

If a campaign is already running and enquiries do not come, the cause is usually one of seven, and each can be checked: [ads running, no enquiries](/en/blog/ads-running-no-leads/).

## What a PPC campaign that sells looks like

An example from [Severské drevo](/en/case/severske-drevo-google-ads/), an online shop selling timber for house and garden:

- each group of materials (beams, boards, decking, thermowood) has its own ads and its own statistics;
- groups that sold almost nothing for months are paused and their budget went where the orders are;
- display banners ran for a year and returned 0.97€ for each euro, less than they cost, so they are stopped;
- each purchase is tracked with the order value, so Google optimises for sales, not clicks.

The result since spring 2025: 324 purchases worth 134,684€ for 18,022€ of spend. None of it is a trick. It is regular work with numbers, which is what a PPC specialist does.

## What a PPC specialist does

At the start:

1. sets up conversion tracking (form, call, purchase) and Consent Mode v2;
2. builds the campaign structure by service or product group;
3. chooses keywords and prepares a basic list of negative keywords;
4. writes the ads and checks that they lead to the right pages.

Every week:

- reads the search terms report and removes what you do not want to pay for;
- adjusts bids and budgets to what brings enquiries;
- tests new ads against old ones;
- checks that tracking still works.

Once a month: figures you can read. What an enquiry cost, how many came, what changed and why.

What to ask when choosing an agency or a freelance PPC specialist: **who owns the Google Ads account** (it should be you, or you lose all the statistics when you part ways), **who sets up tracking and how**, and **what exactly the management includes**: a list of tasks instead of the word "optimisation".
