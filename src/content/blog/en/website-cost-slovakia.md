# What a website costs in Slovakia in 2026: prices by type and what is included

The honest answer to "what does a website cost" sounds dull: it depends what you call a website. A range from 500 to 50,000 euros is not a price range for one service — it is different services sharing a word.

Below, specific figures by type and, more usefully, an explanation of what exactly is being paid for.

## Prices by type

| Type | Price | Who it suits |
|---|---|---|
| **Landing page** | **from 1,500 €** | one service, one offer, advertising for a specific proposition |
| **Company site** | **from 3,500 €** | a company with several services, portfolio, blog |
| **Online shop** | **from 5,000 €** | selling goods with a cart, payment and delivery |

The hourly rate for changes is **35 €**.

Within each category the total is decided by four things: number of pages, number of languages, integrations (CRM, payments, booking, stock) and the amount of bespoke design.

## Why 500-euro offers exist

They do exist, and it is worth knowing what lies behind them: a ready WordPress theme with the text and logo swapped. That is not a scam — for some tasks it works.

The problems start later:

- the template drags a dozen plugins with it, each needing updates
- loading speed suffers, and Google factors it into positions
- the template has none of your mandatory company details, no EU-compliant cookie banner and no enquiry measurement
- attempting to add a non-standard feature reveals the template cannot do it

After a year such a site is usually rebuilt. The final sum comes out higher than doing it properly at the start.

## What the price includes

To compare offers you need to know what can be in a quote at all:

- **a structure built for your tasks**, rather than fitting the business to a template
- **responsive layout** — more than half of visitors arrive by phone
- **forms with spam protection** and a check that the emails actually arrive
- **domain and hosting connected**, email on your own domain
- **mandatory company details** — IČO, DIČ, register entry ([what exactly is required](/en/blog/mandatory-website-details/))
- **a cookie banner with Consent Mode v2** ([why it is mandatory](/en/blog/cookie-banner-2026/))
- **GA4 and Tag Manager analytics** measuring enquiries, not just pageviews
- **basic SEO setup** — headings, markup, sitemap, Search Console
- **six months of technical support** included

The last four items are usually missing from cheap offers — and they are precisely what decides whether the site brings enquiries.

## Costs people forget

The development quote is not the whole cost of owning a site.

**Domain and hosting** — roughly 50–150 € a year. A trifle, but they must be registered to you, not the contractor ([why that matters](/en/blog/sk-domain-guide/)).

**Copy.** Who will write it? If you, budget your own time. If a copywriter, that is a separate and noticeable line.

**Photographs.** Stock images are recognised instantly and work against trust. Your own photographs mean either your time or a photographer.

**A payment gateway**, if you sell: connection plus a percentage of every transaction ([how to choose](/en/blog/accept-card-payments/)).

**Support after launch.** Updates, backups, fixes. With me the first six months are included, then by the hour. Others charge a subscription of 50–200 € a month.

Ask about each item in advance. The difference between "a site for 2,000" and "a site for 3,500" is often explained entirely by this list.

## How to compare offers

Not by the final figure but by three questions:

1. **What is included?** Ask for a list. If it has no analytics, cookie banner or mandatory company details, you are comparing different things.
2. **Who owns the domain, hosting and code?** They should be yours.
3. **What happens after launch?** What a change costs, who fixes a breakage, whether support exists.

An offer with a lower price and no answers to these questions usually turns out dearer.
