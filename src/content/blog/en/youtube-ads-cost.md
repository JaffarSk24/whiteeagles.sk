# YouTube ads: what they cost and who they suit

YouTube is the second largest search engine after Google, and its ads are set up in the same Google Ads as search ads. They are videos before a video, during it, or in YouTube's search results.

Below: which YouTube ad formats exist, what you pay for, what it costs, what video is enough to start and when YouTube does not pay off at all. If you would rather hand it over, see [Google Ads and Meta ads management](/en/service/ads/): launch from 1,000€, management 25€/hr.

## YouTube ad formats

| Format | What it looks like | What you pay for | What it suits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skippable in-stream** | before or during a video, skippable after 5 seconds | a view: 30 seconds, the whole shorter video, or a click | introducing a product, enquiries |
| **Non-skippable** | up to 15 seconds (longer on TV screens), cannot be skipped | a thousand impressions | one clear message |
| **Bumper** | 6 seconds, cannot be skipped | a thousand impressions | brand or event reminders |
| **In-feed** | a video thumbnail in search results and recommendations | a click to play | how-tos, reviews, demos |
| **Shorts** | between short videos on mobile | depends on the campaign | younger audiences, quick demos |

In practice you do not pick the format by hand. In Google Ads you choose the campaign goal, such as sales, leads or reach, and Google offers the formats that belong to it.

## What YouTube ads cost

With skippable ads you pay **only for a view**: when someone watches at least 30 seconds, a shorter video in full, or clicks it. Whoever skips after five seconds costs you nothing and still saw the opening. In Slovakia a view usually costs cents; the reach planner in Google Ads gives a closer estimate for your audience before launch.

Non-skippable and 6-second formats are paid per thousand impressions, so here you buy reach, not a reaction.

**Budget for a first test**: 10-20€ a day for two or three weeks. Less is not enough to show which audience and which video work. You pay the budget straight to Google; setup and management are separate: a campaign launch costs about 1,000€, management 25€/hr.

For comparison, prices in search and Shopping, with figures from my campaigns, are in [PPC advertising](/en/blog/ppc-advertising/).

## Who YouTube suits

- **The product needs to be seen in motion**: a service with a visible result, equipment, a course, a venue, an event.
- **People should remember the brand before they search.** Someone who saw you on YouTube is more likely to click the familiar name in search later.
- **An event with a date.** For the [Astana Ballet](/en/case/astana-ballet-vienna-ads/) performances in Vienna, banners and videos voiced in German, Russian and Slovak ran on the Google network. The first phase brought 21,610 clicks to the ticket pages at 0.027€ each.
- **Retargeting.** For people who visited the site and did nothing, a video reminds them of the offer more cheaply and more vividly than a banner.

## When YouTube does not pay off

- **People actively search for your service** ("oprava práčky bratislava", "účtovník senec"). The money first goes to search, where you pay for people with a ready need.
- **You have no video and nothing to make one from.** A still image with music is weaker on YouTube than the same image in a Facebook feed.
- **The budget covers one campaign.** I usually add YouTube as a second channel once search is running and tracked.

## What video is enough to start

No studio needed. What it needs:

1. **the first five seconds**, before the "Skip" button, make clear what you offer and to whom;
2. the **product or the result** is visible, not a logo on a black background;
3. it is **15 to 30 seconds** long and ends with a clear call to action: call, book, see the offer;
4. it works **without sound**, with subtitles, since many people watch on mobile with the sound off;
5. it exists in **horizontal and vertical** versions if you also want Shorts.

If you speak to several language groups, each gets its own video, as Astana Ballet did. Translated subtitles under a German voice persuade less.

## How a YouTube campaign is launched

1. The video is uploaded to the **company YouTube channel**, unlisted if you prefer it not to appear among the regular videos.
2. The channel is **linked to Google Ads**.
3. In Google Ads a campaign is created with a goal, an **audience** (interests, people who searched for certain words, website visitors), a location and a language.
4. **Tracking** is set up: the enquiry or purchase on the site in GA4, so you can see whether YouTube brings customers rather than just views. How, in [conversion tracking for ads](/en/blog/meta-pixel-conversion-tracking/).
5. After two or three weeks the audiences and videos are compared and the weak ones switched off.
